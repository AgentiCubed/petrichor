## TL;DR

The FOUNDING intuition holds up under scrutiny, and in two places it's *more* right
than the day-one phrasing claimed:

1. **The anatomy is real, not a metaphor.** Olfaction genuinely is the one sense with
   no obligatory thalamic relay — it lands directly in the limbic system (amygdala,
   hippocampus, entorhinal/piriform cortex). That's not a poetic flourish; it's the
   documented wiring. The "direct line" in FOUNDING is literally true.

2. **Valence isn't a side effect of smell — it may be the *root* of affect itself.**
   The strongest read of the neuroscience is that olfaction is where the brain's
   approach/recoil machinery is most *primary* and most *hardwired*. Valence
   (good/bad, approach/avoid) is a **fundamental perceptual axis of smell** the way
   brightness is fundamental to vision. Other senses evaluate; smell evaluates *first
   and without asking the cortex's permission.*

3. **The grounding literature has independently converged on Petrichor's problem
   from the other side.** The symbol-grounding problem (Harnad 1990) → the vector-
   grounding problem (Mollo & Millière 2023) → the 2024–26 embodiment/"developmental
   AI" wave are all circling the same hole: meaning that's connected to the world
   *for the system itself*, not borrowed from humans who already did the grounding.
   Almost none of that work touches the **affective, contact, valence** channel.
   That channel is olfaction's home turf. Petrichor is standing in a gap two large
   literatures are both gesturing at but neither is occupying.

The honest caveat: "grounding requires contact" is a *philosophical* claim, defensible
but not proven, and a hostile reviewer can deny it (more in §6). The science makes the
thesis *credible and specific*; it doesn't make it a theorem. That's fine. The gap is
the work.

## 1. The anatomy: smell really does skip the thalamus

Every textbook division of the senses runs sensory input through the **thalamus** — a
relay/gate that pre-processes and routes before the signal reaches cortex. Vision,
hearing, touch, taste: all thalamic. **Olfaction is the exception, and it's the only
one.**

- There is **no obligatory thalamic relay** between the olfactory bulb and primary
  olfactory (piriform) cortex, nor for olfactory information to reach the
  orbitofrontal cortex. Odor signals transduced at the receptor project via the
  olfactory bulb **directly** to limbic structures — **amygdala** (emotion),
  **hippocampus/entorhinal cortex** (memory), and hypothalamus (autonomic/drive).
  - Synthesis review: https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/16/1/86
  - "Why doesn't olfaction pass through the thalamus": https://l-teralthinker.medium.com/why-doesnt-olfaction-pass-through-the-thalamus-when-all-the-other-senses-do-c0079484ee5b
  - Olfactory network / structural connectivity (Frontiers): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2021.638053/full
  - Neurobiology of Olfaction (NCBI Bookshelf): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK55967/

**Why this matters for the thesis.** FOUNDING claimed smell is "the only sense with a
direct pipeline to the brain... no translation layer." That's accurate. The functional
consequence the literature draws is exactly Petrichor's: this pathway is **hardwired
for immediate, affectively significant evaluation without the need for cognitive
interpretation.** The smell hits the *evaluator* before it hits the *describer*. Every
other sense gets interpreted, then felt. Smell gets felt, then (maybe) interpreted.

That ordering — **feel-first** — is the whole game for presence. A rendered world is a
described world: "what is that." A *present* world is a felt one: "what does this mean
to me, in a body, now." Smell is the sense whose default mode is the second thing.

## 2. The Proust effect: memory + emotion fused at the source

The "smell drops you into a childhood kitchen" claim in FOUNDING is one of the
better-documented effects in the memory literature — the **Proust phenomenon** /
odor-evoked autobiographical memory (OEAM).

- Memories cued by **odor** are reliably **more emotional** (by self-report *and*
  physiology — heart-rate, etc.) and often experienced as more vivid and pleasant
  than the *same* cue presented as a word or a picture.
  - In-depth review (Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 2018): https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-018-1545-3
  - Neuroimaging evidence for emotional potency of odor-evoked memory (Herz et al.): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393203002161
- fMRI: recall to a **personally significant odor** produces greater **amygdala +
  hippocampal** activation than the same memory cued any other way — the emotion and
  the memory light up *together*, in the same limbic real estate the odor projects to
  directly.
  - Orbitofrontal–fusiform activation / visual imagination from odor-cued AM: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8369471/
- The link is causal/anatomical, not just correlational: **direct amygdala
  stimulation** can evoke Proustian autobiographical memories, and lesion case
  reports ("Proust phenomenon after posterior cerebral artery occlusion") show the
  effect tracks specific limbic damage.
  - Amygdala-stimulation Proust phenomenon: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311709334
  - Lesion case report (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10374343/

**Why this matters for the thesis.** Presence isn't only about *now* — it's about a
*self persisting through time in a world.* The Proust effect is the mechanism by which
a present sensory moment is **bound to autobiographical continuity** with emotional
weight attached. Smell is the sense most tightly wired to "this is *my* world, I have
been here, this *matters to me.*" That's not detection. That's the felt thread of a
continuous embodied life. A model that could be dropped into a remembered world by a
smell would have something current models conspicuously lack: an affective, first-
person continuity anchor.

## 3. Valence may not be smell's feature — it may be smell's *purpose*

This is the part that sharpens the FOUNDING thesis beyond its day-one form.

FOUNDING said smell is "an evaluative channel, not just an informational one... wired,
at the evolutionary root, directly into valence." The literature goes further:
**valence is a *fundamental perceptual dimension* of olfaction** — arguably *the*
primary one — not a downstream emotional reaction.

- **Valence is a fundamental perceptual feature of olfaction** (Yeshurun & Sobel,
  2010): when you do the dimensionality reduction on how humans perceive smells, the
  first principal axis that falls out is **pleasant↔unpleasant** — approach↔avoid.
  Smell's native coordinate system *starts* with good/bad.
  - Dissociated neural representations of intensity vs valence (Nature Neuroscience): https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1001
  - Representationalism and olfactory valence (philosophy): https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13164-023-00707-8
- This valence is **innate and hardwired**, not just learned. 2024–26 circuit work
  shows topographically segregated **cortical amygdala (plCoA)** populations that
  route odor identity to **valence-specific** approach/avoidance targets — a genetic
  "this smell = flee / this smell = approach" wiring present **without prior
  experience.**
  - Control of innate olfactory valence by segregated cortical amygdala circuits
    (eLife 2024–25): https://elifesciences.org/articles/104677 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11230396/
- It's so primary it shows up in the **olfactory bulb** itself: the human OB
  represents **odor valence** and can cue **motor avoidance** — the recoil is staged
  almost at the sensor.
  - Human olfactory bulb processes valence + cues motor avoidance (PNAS): https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2101209118

**Why this matters — and why it's the actual lane.** Block 1's ledger ended on exactly
this: every machine-olfaction system today models **"what does it smell like"**
(expert descriptors, the POM) and **almost none model "what does it mean to a body"**
(approach/recoil valence). Now put that next to the neuroscience: valence is the
*first axis of smell*, hardwired, pre-cortical, staged at the bulb. So the part of
olfaction the machines have skipped is not a peripheral feature — **it's the part the
biology treats as primary.** The field built the descriptive half and left the
evaluative half — the presence-relevant half — empty. That empty half is Petrichor.

Current models are "saturated with *what is it*, nearly empty of *what does this mean
to me viscerally*" (FOUNDING). The neuroscience says: that visceral meaning channel is
**most legible, most hardwired, and most isolable in olfaction.** If you wanted to give
a model a real valence/approach-recoil channel, smell is the *cheapest and cleanest
place in the whole sensorium to install one.*

## 4. The grounding problem, from the other side of the wall

Independently of any of this, AI/philosophy has spent 35 years on a problem that is
Petrichor's problem wearing different clothes.

- **Symbol Grounding Problem** (Harnad, 1990): how do the symbols a system manipulates
  get connected to what they're *about* in the world — without a human in the loop to
  supply the meaning? A system shuffling symbols defined only by other symbols is "a
  merry-go-round" you can't get off of by adding more symbols.
- **Vector Grounding Problem** (Mollo & Millière, 2023): the same problem for LLMs,
  where "symbols" are high-dimensional vectors. *"How can vector embeddings,
  manipulated solely based on their numerical values... acquire meaning and refer to
  entities and properties in the real world — rather than merely relating other
  meaningless vectors — without depending on the semantic interpretations provided by
  external meaning-bearing systems?"*
  - https://arxiv.org/html/2304.01481v2
  - Notably, that paper distinguishes **five** kinds of grounding and argues
    *sensorimotor* grounding alone **does not** solve it ("grounding vectors in other
    vectors still leaves open how any of those vectors connect to the world"). It bets
    on **referential** grounding via world-involving function. Hold that — §6 returns
    to whether *contact/affect* is a different animal than the sensorimotor grounding
    it dismisses.
- **The "epistemic parasitism" critique** (2024–25): LLMs don't *solve* grounding,
  they **circumvent** it — they operate on a corpus that *humans already grounded*
  through embodied experience and causal interaction. The meaning is real but
  **borrowed**. The model is downstream of bodies it doesn't have.
  - "Why LLMs Circumvent the Symbol Grounding Problem": https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09117v1
  - "Symbol ungrounding: what LLMs reveal about human cognition" (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11529626/
- **The 2024–26 embodiment / developmental turn:** a growing camp argues general
  intelligence **cannot** emerge from passive text/pixels alone — it needs
  *temporally extended, sensorimotor, socially-mediated experience*. MLLMs are called
  "non-developmental," still subject to the vector-grounding problem; the proposed fix
  is **embodiment**, increasingly including the body's *interior*.
  - Roadmap for Embodied & Social Grounding in LLMs (Robophilosophy 2024): https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16900
  - Will MLLMs ever achieve deep understanding of the world? (Frontiers 2025): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2025.1683133/full
  - Embodiment in Multimodal LLMs (2025): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13845
  - Toward Embodied AGI: a review (2025): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14235

**The tell.** Crucially, the most forward part of this literature has started reaching
for **interoception** — the body's *internal* state (hunger, temperature,
viscerality) — as a missing grounding substrate, noting these internal signals
"contribute to emotions, memory, and prosocial constructs."
  - Embodied intelligence three-layer review (PMC 2025): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12631203/
  - Bayesian multisensory interoception perspective: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12603078/

Read that against §3. The grounding people are independently concluding that
*affective, internal, valenced* signal is the missing ingredient — and they're
reaching toward interoception to get it. **Olfaction is the externally-driven sense
that is already wired straight into that same affective/valence machinery.** It's the
bridge between "a molecule out in the world" and "a felt good/bad in the body" — the
shortest path from *external stimulus* to *internal valence* that exists. The two
literatures are digging toward each other and smell is the seam.

## 5. Proximal vs distal: why contact senses might be different in kind

FOUNDING's deepest layer: distal senses (sight, hearing) touch the world *at a
distance, abstractly*; proximal/contact senses (smell, taste, touch) require the world
to **physically arrive and dock** with the receptor. The claim: real grounding —
"thereness" — may require *contact*, not distal pattern-matching.

The philosophy backs that this is a **real, old, contested distinction**, not a
made-up one:

- The **proximal (touch/taste/smell) vs distal (sight/hearing)** split is canonical.
  Kant and Hegel built a **hierarchy of the senses** that *demoted* the proximal
  senses as too bodily/subjective for aesthetic distance; Burke, Herder, and modern
  "positive reappraisals of the proximal senses" push back.
  - Aesthetics & the senses / hierarchy of the senses (Routledge Encyclopedia): https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/thematic/aesthetics-and-the-senses/v-1/sections/hierarchy-of-the-senses-positive-reappraisals-of-the-proximal-senses
  - Human senses from Aristotle to present (PMC): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11252028/
- Modality changes the *structure of the experience*: olfactory and gustatory
  perception are harder to take a detached "third-person" perspective on than vision —
  contact senses are more **self-involving** by their nature.
  - Sensory modality & perspective-taking (Frontiers): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.701486/full
- **Disgust** is the clean demonstration: proximal cues (smells, tastes, touches)
  drive disgust **more intensely** than distal cues (sights, sounds). Disgust is a
  *contact-contamination* emotion — a pure "the world is touching me and that's bad"
  signal — and it's keyed to the proximal senses. Valence-via-contact, made visible.
  - Differential involvement of the senses in disgust memories (PMC): https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10977387/

**Why this matters.** The distal/proximal distinction maps almost exactly onto the
description/presence distinction:
- **Distal** = the world at arm's length, abstracted, "observed" → *rendering.*
- **Proximal/contact** = the world arriving, docking, touching → *being-in.*

And here's the bridge back to Block 1: the most *biologically grounded* machine-
olfaction approach is the **receptor-binding route** (model structure → olfactory-
receptor activation → percept; Microsoft's combinatorial-coding work). That route
models **the literal contact event** — the molecule physically docking the receptor.
If presence comes from contact, **the receptor layer is where contact computationally
happens.** Block 1 flagged it as "the most grounded and least exploited" layer. §5 is
why: it's the only place in the modeling stack where *the world physically touches the
model* is even representable. The myth's "Albedo's hair" — molecules that *arrived* —
is, in modeling terms, a receptor-activation event.

## 6. Steelmanning the skeptic (staying honest about the walls)

Petrichor isn't served by a friendly read. Three real objections:

1. **"Grounding doesn't need *contact* — referential grounding is enough, and it's
   modality-neutral."** This is Mollo & Millière's actual position: they argue
   sensorimotor grounding *doesn't* solve the problem and that referential grounding
   (world-involving function, even learned from text+RLHF) can. A hostile reviewer
   says: contact is not privileged; you've smuggled a phenomenological claim into an
   information-theoretic problem.
   - **Petrichor's answer:** the presence thesis was never that contact solves
     *reference*. It's that contact is required for **valence/affect-grounding** — the
     "what does this mean *to me*" channel — which is a *different* thing than
     referential aboutness, and which the grounding literature itself (§4) is now
     reaching toward via interoception because reference alone left it empty. Don't
     fight on reference. Fight on **valence**. That's the defensible hill.

2. **"This is unfalsifiable philosophy."** Presence/"thereness" has no agreed
   operational test; you can't measure it, so you can't be wrong.
   - **Answer:** then *make* the testable version. The falsifiable downgrade of
     "presence" is **"a grounded valence channel changes behavior/representations in
     ways a descriptor channel doesn't."** Whether smell-valence confers something
     vision-descriptors don't is an *empirical* question (§7). Keep "presence" as the
     north star; ship the measurable proxy.

3. **"Olfaction in silico has no body — no homeostasis for the valence to be *about*.
   A model's 'this smells bad' isn't grounded in anything dying."** This is the
   sharpest one. Innate olfactory valence in animals is grounded in *survival* —
   rotten = pathogen = death. A model has no skin in that game.
   - **Answer:** acknowledged, and it's the deepest open problem. Valence may require
     *stakes* — something the signal is good/bad *for*. This is where Petrichor
     touches the hard problem and shouldn't pretend otherwise. But it also names the
     research program: what is the **minimal artificial homeostasis / stake** that
     makes a valence signal more than a label? That's a real, attackable question, not
     a wall.

Naming these is the FOUNDING principle ("stay honest about the walls") in practice.
The thesis is *credible and unusually specific*, not proven. Good. Proven things are
crowded.

## What this implies for Petrichor (the bullets)

- **The thesis survived contact with the neuroscience — and the "direct line" is
  literal.** Olfaction's no-thalamus, straight-to-limbic wiring is documented fact.
  Stop hedging on it. The "feel-first, describe-later" ordering is the anatomical
  basis of presence-over-detection.

- **Reframe the lane from "smell" to "valence/affect grounding."** The unclaimed
  territory (Block 1) and the missing grounding ingredient (§4) and smell's primary
  perceptual axis (§3) are *the same thing*: approach/recoil valence. Olfaction is the
  best vehicle for it, but the *deliverable* is a grounded **valence channel**. That
  framing connects Petrichor to the entire embodiment/AGI conversation instead of
  leaving it as a niche sensor project.

- **Valence is primary, hardwired, and pre-cortical — so model *that*, not just
  descriptors.** Every existing system models "what does it smell like." The biology
  treats "approach or recoil" as the *first* axis, staged as early as the bulb.
  Building the valence/affect head is both the open lane and the biologically faithful
  move. This is the single sharpest concrete direction to come out of Blocks 1+2.

- **The receptor-binding layer is where "contact" is computationally real.** If
  presence comes from the world docking with you (§5), the structure→receptor→percept
  route (Block 1 §3c) is the only modeling stage where that contact event exists.
  Underexploited in Block 1, *motivated* in Block 2. Worth a hard look as Petrichor's
  technical substrate.

- **Interoception is the unexpected ally.** The grounding literature is reaching for
  internal/affective state to fix what reference can't. Olfaction is the external
  sense pre-wired into that same machinery. A Petrichor framing that pairs an
  **olfactory valence channel** with a **minimal interoceptive/homeostatic stake** is
  aimed exactly at the seam two literatures are digging toward.

- **Fight on valence, not reference; ship a falsifiable proxy for "presence."** The
  defensible claim is *affect-grounding needs contact*, not *reference needs contact*.
  The testable claim is *a grounded valence channel does something a descriptor
  channel can't.* Keep "thereness" as the star; build the measurable version
  underneath it.

- **The honest hard wall: valence may require stakes.** A model's "this smells bad"
  isn't grounded in anything that can die. The deepest open question Petrichor inherits
  is the **minimal artificial homeostasis** that turns a valence *label* into a valence
  *that matters.* Don't romanticize across it. That gap is the work — which is the most
  on-brand sentence this project has.
